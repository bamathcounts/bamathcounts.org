2026–27 Competition Series

Your path to participating.

Registration is through the MATHCOUNTS Foundation. Students join through their school or, if eligible, through a parent or guardian.

Students & parents

Start by asking your math teacher or school administration whether your school participates. The school’s coach selects its chapter competitors; registration is not a walk-in ticket.

If your school will not register or support participation through the school, a parent or guardian can register you as a non-school competitor (NSC). Confirm this with school officials first. NSCs compete individually, not on a team.

A maximum of 14 NSCs from the same school may register, first come, first served. Home-school students normally register through their home school, not as NSCs; virtual-school students follow the official virtual-school rules.

Read eligibility rules ↗ (opens in a new tab)

Starting a school program

  1. Choose a school representative. A teacher, administrator or parent volunteer with the school administration’s permission can register.
  2. Confirm your chapter. Use our three chapter listings to check your school’s assignment before choosing its chapter during registration.
  3. Register with the Foundation. Create an account or sign in, then register up to one team of four and ten individuals. Any number of students can practice at school.
  4. Start practicing. Use past competition papers and the registered coach’s School Handbook.
  5. Finalize your roster. Identify team members and individuals in the Coach Dashboard before your chapter’s roster lockout. Confirm the current lockout with your coordinator.

Plan your visit

Competition-day essentials.

Known national rules, with local arrangements still to come.

What to bring

  • You may bring pencils, pens and erasers. Organizers provide pencils and paper; do not bring your own scratch or graph paper.
  • Bring a permitted calculator for Target and Team (and any tiebreaker). Calculators cannot have a QWERTY keypad, Wi-Fi or wireless adapters. Phones, tablets and other smart devices cannot substitute for calculators.
  • No calculators in Sprint or Countdown. Notes and reference materials are not permitted.
Equipment rules ↗ (opens in a new tab)

How the day works

The national format is designed to take about three hours. This is not a confirmed local start-to-finish schedule.

Plan for check-in, written rounds, any local Countdown Round, and recognition of winning students and teams. Round order, check-in and awards times will be announced with each chapter’s schedule.

Parents and guests: waiting areas, spectator access and whether parents can stay are not yet confirmed. Check the event notice or ask your chapter coordinator before planning your visit.

For disability-related accommodations, contact the coordinator in writing at least three weeks before the competition, following the official rules.

More from MATHCOUNTS ↗ (opens in a new tab)
What is MATHCOUNTS?+

MATHCOUNTS is a national middle school math program. The Competition Series serves students in grades 6–8, progressing through school, chapter, state and national levels. You do not need to be an expert to start practicing: try a few past problems and ask your math teacher about joining.

When will dates and venues be announced?+

The national chapter competition window is February 2027. Local dates and venues are not yet confirmed for this website. We will post each chapter’s details when confirmed; no announcement date has been set. Do not assume all three chapters run on the same day. Updates and coordinator contacts ↓

How do I join if my school has no coach?+

Ask your school administration whether a teacher or an approved parent volunteer can start a program. If the school will not register or support participation through the school, a parent or guardian may register an eligible non-school competitor. Confirm with school officials first. See the school and NSC steps ↓

Do individuals take the Team Round?+

No. School team members take Sprint, Target and Team. Students registered as individuals, including NSCs, take Sprint and Target, not Team. All competitors can qualify for Countdown if it is offered. A school team can have up to four students; up to ten additional students can compete as individuals.

How do students advance to state?+

The winning chapter team and at least the two highest-ranked competitors who are not on that team advance to state. The state coordinator may allow additional teams or individuals to advance. The exact additional qualifying policy is not yet published here.

What should we bring, and can parents stay?+

See our competition-day essentials for equipment and calculator rules. Local check-in times, guest access, waiting areas and the final schedule will be announced with the event details.

Can high school students volunteer or earn service hours?+

Local age requirements, service-hour documentation, shifts and training arrangements are still being confirmed. Ask your chapter coordinator before signing up or relying on service credit. Local volunteer guidance ↓
